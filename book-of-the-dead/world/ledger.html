Filling the gapsThe sourcing workstream

Scott's instruction (2026-10-06): a complete, coherent world with no gaps, filled from alternative sources, cited. Nothing enters the model from outside the Papyrus of Ani until it has passed four steps.

  1. Sourcing. For each gap below, a candidate passage is located in a named public-domain edition: manuscript, spell, edition, volume, page, with a link to the scan or transcription. Astra (ChatGPT) does the locating and transcribing and ships the result as a package with SHA-256 hashes, as for the study.
  2. Translation. The passage is taken in a public-domain English translation (Budge 1898 or 1905, Renouf and Naville 1904). Where only a copyrighted modern translation exists, it is paraphrased and cited, never reproduced.
  3. Cross-examination. Every physical description that would shape the model is compared across at least two translations (Budge against Renouf, and against Faulkner or Allen where they differ). Disagreement on a dimension, a count or a material downgrades the feature to contested, and the model shows the contest.
  4. Review. Before a passage enters this ledger, a fresh check confirms it exists at the cited location, by opening the source. Figures get the plain second check. Then the feature is built, labelled with its source class: witness for another Book of the Dead manuscript, cognate for a related composition such as the Amduat or the Book of Gates.
